Cool the Cluster

Which hall design does the most GPU work per kWh, and what is wearing out in a hall that's already running? One simulator answers both. It scores designs so agents can search for better ones, and it reads sensor data to predict failures and rank fixes.

hall 30 racks · 768 GPUs · ~1 MW best design 3.5% less energy per unit of work tracker 5 of 5 planted faults found sourced optics, switches, plant from the team catalog

How it works

input

A hall design

One JSON file covering rack slots, optics, setpoints, containment and pipes.

→
twin

Simulate a year

Summer, winter, an inference peak, a mixture-of-experts (MoE) training job, and the same hall after 5 years of aging. Any broken limit rejects the design.

→
output

kWh per unit of GPU work

Throttling or a slow network lowers work, so they can't fake savings.

What this does not prove

  • The tracker test is circular. The same model generated the sensor data and read it back, so 5/5 is a pipeline check, not field accuracy. Real BMS and optics telemetry is the next test.
  • Hall physics is estimated. 18 of 302 numbers (optics, switches, UPS, air handlers, CDU) are vendor-sourced from the team catalog. Recirculation, chiller, rack power and prices are not yet. Good for ranking designs, not for quoting a real PUE.
  • 3.5% is small at this density. The benchmark rack is ~38 kW; catalog racks are 130–245 kW, where liquid, CDU and floor limits dominate. Server power dominates; cooling and network are about 10% of the load.
  • No airflow simulation (CFD), humidity or N+1 redundancy.
